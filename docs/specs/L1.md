# QBC Workboard — L1 Product Requirements

## 1. Purpose

QBC Workboard shall be a deliverable, responsive web application for Quinntyne Brown Consulting Inc. It shall support planning and executing software, product-development, and AI work in two-week Scrum sprints. The application shall replace the browser-local behavior in `docs/mocks` with a persistent backend and an Angular frontend while preserving the mock's simple, spacious user experience.

The keywords **shall**, **should**, and **may** indicate mandatory, recommended, and optional behavior respectively.

## 2. Product requirements

### L1-001 — Workspace navigation

The product shall provide a coherent workspace from which a user can navigate among the active sprint board, backlog, initiative hierarchy, and assistant directory.

### L1-002 — Work hierarchy

The product shall let a user create, view, update, and delete initiatives and epics, and shall maintain the hierarchy Initiative → Epic → Story.

### L1-003 — Stories and tasks

The product shall let a user create, view, update, archive, restore, and delete stories, including story details, estimates, ownership, lifecycle, and optional checklist tasks.

### L1-004 — Assistants and assignments

The product shall let a user create, view, update, and delete assistants and assign assistants to stories and story tasks.

### L1-005 — Backlog grooming

The product shall provide a searchable and filterable backlog in which draft work can be completed, groomed, marked Ready, and prepared for sprint planning.

### L1-006 — Two-week sprint planning

The product shall let a user create, view, update, start, complete, and, where permitted, delete 14-calendar-day sprints with explicit sprint goals and planned stories.

### L1-007 — Sprint execution

The product shall provide a three-column active-sprint board with To do, In progress, and Done workflow states, visible sprint progress, and efficient story movement.

### L1-008 — Durable and consistent data

The product shall persist work-management data beyond browser sessions and server restarts and shall protect the hierarchy, assignments, and sprint history from invalid or destructive changes.

### L1-009 — Responsive and accessible experience

The product shall provide a radically simple, spacious, responsive, keyboard-accessible experience that remains usable on desktop, tablet, and mobile viewports.

### L1-015 — Logged hours

The product shall let a user record the hours an assistant spent on a story, correct that record by removing an entry, and read, for a single assistant, the stories they worked on, the hours logged against each, and how much of their logged time is on stories that are now complete. An assistant shall be understood to have worked on a story when they have hours logged against it, because story ownership names only who holds a story now. Every recorded hour shall be attributed to an assistant chosen when the time is recorded; `L1-013` establishes no individual identity, so the product has none to infer.

## 3. Implementation requirements

### L1-010 — .NET backend architecture

The backend shall be implemented under `backend/` using .NET and Clean Architecture, controller-based HTTP APIs, the last freely licensed version of the MediatR library (`MediatR` 12.5.0), and one declared type per source file.

### L1-011 — Angular frontend architecture

The frontend shall be implemented under `frontend/` using Angular, separate TypeScript/template/style files, Signals as the primary UI state mechanism, one declared type per source file, and interface-driven service consumption through typed Angular injection tokens.

### L1-012 — Product quality

All executable product behavior and logic shall be implemented using acceptance test-driven development (ATDD). Each change shall begin with an acceptance test that fails for the expected missing behavior—either a backend integration test or a frontend Playwright end-to-end test using the Page Object Model—followed by the radically simplest implementation that makes the test pass. The passing acceptance tests shall remain as the product's regression suite.

### L1-013 — Temporary workspace passcode gate

The deployed product shall require a single shared passcode before any work-management resource is readable or changeable, and shall issue a time-limited session credential once that passcode is accepted. The gate shall throttle repeated passcode attempts. It shall not be presented as user authentication: it establishes no individual identity, no per-person authorization, and no audit of who made a change.

### L1-014 — Deployed build identification

The deployed product shall report the backend and frontend builds it is serving, and shall present both identities in the workspace. Each identity shall be derived from its own deployed artifact rather than from runtime configuration, shall name the commit that artifact was built from when the build pipeline supplied one, and shall be readable without the `L1-013` passcode.

## 4. Scope boundaries and assumptions

- The initial product is a single QBC workspace. `L1-013` adds one shared passcode gate in front of it. Individual user accounts, per-person authorization, multiple organizations, notifications, comments, attachments, and external integrations remain outside this requirements baseline. `L1-015` brings recorded hours inside it, attributed to an assistant named when the time is recorded rather than to a signed-in person.
- Initiative and epic archival are outside scope; these records support create, read, update, and guarded permanent deletion.
- Story task management is intentionally lightweight: title, completion state, and optional assistant assignee.
- The mock's seeded sample records and **Reset demo** action are prototype aids, not production features.
- The production system shall use server-side persistence; browser storage shall not be the system of record. The `L1-013` session credential is the sole exception: it is a credential, not a product record, and is discarded when it expires.
- A completed sprint is retained as immutable history except for corrections to its display name or goal; its completed story membership shall not be replanned.
- The ATDD requirement applies to executable product behavior and logic. Dedicated tests shall not be created to assert folder structure, file naming or type counts, dependency direction, framework or package selection, formatting, documentation, or other code-organization constraints; those constraints shall be verified by review and normal build or static-analysis tooling.
